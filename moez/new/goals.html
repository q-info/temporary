Research, Teaching, and Software Development Agenda

Research Vision

My research explores how mathematical structures — particularly algebraic, categorical, graph-theoretic, order-theoretic, and (co)inductive structures — can clarify the foundations of computation and support the design, analysis, and implementation of new computational methods, with a particular focus on quantum computation. A complementary objective is to develop interactive computational environments that make these structures explorable, testable, and useful for both research and education.

Central Research Question

How can mathematical structure and interactive computational exploration be used to develop better models, algorithms, and software for computation, particularly quantum computation?

Research Goals

A. Quantum Algorithms and Quantum Informatics

  • Investigate the computational structure of graph isomorphism from a quantum algorithmic perspective, including whether quantum methods can provide meaningful advantages for general graphs or important graph classes.

  • Study connections between graph isomorphism and the non-commutative Hidden Subgroup Problem (ncHSP), drawing on spectral graph theory, algebraic graph theory, representation-theoretic ideas, and structural properties of quantum operations.

  • Identify recursive, self-similar, categorical, or other structural regularities in quantum operations that may support new algorithmic constructions or more efficient analysis.

  • Use interactive and visual experimentation—particularly through QAL and the QA-LA NIVs visual engine—to formulate conjectures, inspect algorithmic structure, compare representations, and support reproducible computational exploration.

B. Mathematical Models of Quantum Phenomena

  • Develop and evaluate alternative mathematical descriptions of quantum entanglement, including models based on partial products and related algebraic structures.

  • Explore whether mutual coinduction and infinite coinductive constructions provide useful models for interference, entanglement, superposition, measurement, or the dynamics of quantum processes.

  • Clarify the relationships among categorical, algebraic, order-theoretic, and coinductive models of quantum computation and identify cases in which one representation offers a practical analytical or computational advantage.

C. Programming Languages and Formal Models of Computation

  • Publish a concise domain theory primer that supports students and researchers entering semantics and mathematical models of programming languages.

  • Further develop GNOOP as a category-theoretic model of nominally typed object-oriented generics as found in languages such as Java, C#, Scala, and Kotlin.

  • Investigate conceptual and technical connections between categorical models used in programming language semantics and those used in quantum computation, including opportunities for cross fertilization between type systems, semantics, and quantum programming.

D. Research Infrastructure and Applied Exploration

  • Develop the proposed Quantum Informatics Lab (QI Lab) at the Informatics Research Institute (IRI), SRTA-City, as a platform for research, student training, software development, and external collaboration.

  • Use QI Lab and QAL to evaluate applied quantum computing scenarios in which optimization, simulation, scheduling, security, or data analysis may benefit from quantum or hybrid quantum-classical methods.

  • Develop the proposed Quantum FIFA World Cup 2034 concept as a clearly scoped case study for evaluating a broad quantum informatics ecosystem, rather than as a standalone claim of quantum advantage. Candidate use cases should be defined, benchmarked against classical baselines, and assessed for technological readiness and practical value.

Teaching Goals

  • Develop a coherent teaching pathway in quantum computing, quantum algorithms, programming languages, and supporting mathematics for graduate, undergraduate, and advanced STEM/STEAM students.

  • Integrate QAL-based interactive laboratories, visualizations, and computational exercises into courses so that students can move from conceptual understanding to algorithm construction and experimentation.

  • Create modular learning materials that can be used in short courses, workshops, semester courses, invited lectures, and outreach programs.

  • Use teaching as a pipeline into research by identifying suitable student projects that contribute to QAL, QI Lab, quantum algorithms research, and formal models of computation.

  • Curate selected material from the Quantum Informatics, Algorithms, and Programming (QIAP) channel into durable, structured educational resources.

Software Development Goals

Priority principle: Software development should directly support the research and teaching agenda, with clear milestones and research outputs.

Priority 1 — QAL as a Research Platform

  • Strengthen the core QAL architecture as an interactive environment for representing, constructing, analyzing, and teaching quantum algorithms.

  • Build QAL Lib as a curated and testable library of quantum algorithms, examples, reusable components, and benchmark cases.

  • Extend the QA-LA NIVs visual engine to support richer mathematical and algorithmic representations, comparison of alternative formulations, and research grade experimentation.

  • Document QAL sufficiently for reproducibility, external use, student contributions, and research collaboration.

Priority 2 — Immersive and Educational Extensions

  • Develop QAL VR as an experimental interface for totally immersive exploration of quantum algorithms and experimentation with their underlying mathematical structures, while evaluating whether VR provides measurable educational or research benefits beyond conventional visualization.

  • Activate and develop QAL Game Mode to support guided discovery, problem solving, and game-based learning of quantum concepts.

Priority 3 — Exploratory Software Projects

  • Prototype recreational smartphone games inspired by mathematical, logical, and spatial puzzle systems when they provide a useful vehicle for algorithmic or educational experimentation.

  • Develop Android Proof Maker (APM) as a mobile adaptation of Proof Designer, subject to available development capacity and its alignment with the broader research and teaching program.
