Executive Summary

Quantum technologies are promising emerging technologies that have the potential to benefit humanity immensely, and to significantly transform life on earth, and beyond. Researching quantum informatics enables quantum technologies to reach their full potential.
The Quantum Informatics Lab (QI Lab) we propose will provide a unique opportunity for interdisciplinary research and collaboration in quantum information science, quantum algorithms, and quantum computing. With a strong team, state-of-the-art supporting equipment, and a clear vision, we can advance the field and drive innovation in quantum technologies, with particular focus on algorithmic innovation and accessible quantum education tools.
QI Lab has the potential to become a leading center for quantum research, education, and innovation, driving advancements in science, technology, and economic growth.

Introduction

Quantum technologies attempt to exploit the peculiar laws of quantum physics to benefit humanity. For example, quantum computing tries to leverage quantum mechanical principles–such as superposition, entanglement, and interference–to speedup some computations that are currently out of our reach. Quantum technologies currently face multiple hurdles to reach their potential, including quantum hardware challenges and quantum software challenges.
We propose building the Quantum Informatics Lab (QI Lab) at the Informatics Research Institute (IRI), SRTA-City, to particularly address the software challenges facing quantum computing.
Quantum Informatics (QI) is the study of how quantum principles underpin and enhance information processing, representation, transmission, and computation. QI overlaps with Quantum Information Science (QIS) but “informatics” often emphasizes algorithmic and systems-level aspects more.
QI is central to multiple fields, such as quantum computing since it helps find faster algorithms (a main focus of QI Lab) and new complexity classes, quantum communication since QI helps, for example, in reaching secure messaging immune to eavesdropping, quantum sensing since QI helps doing ultra-precise measurement beyond classical limits, and quantum foundations since QI helps reach deep insights into information, reality, and quantum mechanics.
As a research and development (R&D) lab, QI Lab will be focused on researching quantum informatics by conducting experiments on circuit-based quantum algorithms, with a particular focus on developing efficient quantum algorithms for solving complex real-world problems.
The goal of this proposal is to establish the Quantum Informatics Lab as a cutting-edge hub for research, education (a.k.a., pedagogy), and development in quantum information science and related technologies. This lab will provide a platform for interdisciplinary collaboration among researchers and students of computer science, mathematics, and physics (and, in the future, industry professionals) to explore the potential of quantum technologies. The mission of the lab is to develop innovative quantum and classical solutions, and to educate the next generation of quantum scientists and software engineers.
This proposal aims to establish QI Lab as a pioneering academic and research center focused on quantum informatics and quantum algorithms, especially in the Arab and African regions.

Needs Assessment

As its name indicates, Quantum Informatics Lab (QI Lab) will be focused on researching quantum informatics. Egypt, and the Egyptian government, are strongly interested in emerging technologies (See ‘Egypt Vision 2030’), including quantum technologies, and their potential in solving many real-world problems. Yet, no research lab with such focus on researching quantum informatics and quantum algorithms exists in Egypt so far, in spite of similar labs existing elsewhere (e.g., QICDT at Edinburgh Univ., Scotland

https://informatics.ed.ac.uk/quantum-informatics
; QICL at EPFL, Switzerland, QuIC Lab at Raman Research Institute, India; ACQuIRe at UMass Amherst, USA; QIQCL at SNU, South Korea; and others).
Our proposal aims to fill in this gap.

Goals and Objectives

The goals of QI Lab @ IRI will be:
  1. Researching quantum informatics and quantum algorithms to help develop real-world quantum applications that benefit humanity, and
  2. Teaching quantum informatics and quantum algorithms to budding researchers who will help develop such applications.
The lab will also focus on:
  1. Developing research and educational software (such as QAL. See below) to help reach these goals, and
  2. Developing novel software technology inventions (such as QA-LA NIVs. See below) for use in research and educational software.
QI Lab members will also be strongly involved in community building, via contacting and reaching out to similar and related labs and research groups, inside Egypt (see below) and outside (e.g., Rice University, USA, and others), and interacting with young and senior researchers, students, and professionals working in quantum algorithms and informatics.

Scope of Work

Research Focus Areas

  1. Quantum Algorithms Development: Develop new quantum algorithms and improve existing ones, particularly for applications such as logistics, optimization, simulation, and machine learning, via researching their underlying theoretical foundations (e.g., graph isomorphism, and the non-commutative hidden subgroup problem (ncHSP)), ... etc.
  2. Quantum Information Science: Investigate the fundamental principles of quantum information science, including quantum entanglement, quantum interference, quantum non-commutativity, and quantum error correction.
  3. Quantum Computing: Explore the potential of quantum computing for solving complex problems in fields like chemistry, materials science, cryptography, ... etc.

Application Areas

As hinted to above, real-world application areas that QI Lab may focus on developing efficient quantum algorithms for include, but are not limited to, quantum AI/machine learning (QAI), quantum cryptography, quantum image processing, quantum simulation (chemistry, physics, and materials science). As such, QI Lab will have the potential to help solve complex problems in various fields like medicine, artificial intelligence, and materials science.
QI Lab will also directly support national digital transformation goals and may enable breakthrough applications across finance, logistics, healthcare, and cybersecurity.

Core Activities

  1. Research: Focused on studying and finding efficient quantum algorithms, visual tools, and hybrid classical AI-QC systems. Advancing research in efficient quantum algorithms and their mathematical foundations (e.g., linear algebra, group theory, graph theory). Leveraging AI/ML tools to support quantum software discovery and optimization.
  2. Teaching: Quantum computing courses (undergrad & grad), workshops, and competitions. Teaching quantum computing through interactive, visual tools like QAL and QAL-Lite.
  3. Development: Developing and deploying tools like QAL, QAL-Lite, and interactive XR/AR/VR platforms for intuitive quantum learning.
  4. Outreach: Collaborate with schools, universities, companies, local and global partners.

Personnel

The lab will seek to start small and gradually grow bigger over time. It will be headed by one person, and will initially need relatively little financial resources.
To conduct research, teaching, development, and experiments, QI Lab will house specialized personnel, and when necessary, specialized equipment. In particular, QI Lab personnel will be composed, mainly, of faculty and science students (graduate, science and engineering undergraduate, STEM/STEAM).

Lab Structure

  1. Principal Investigator/Lab Director: Experienced researcher in quantum computing and quantum information science.
  2. Postdoctoral Researchers: Researchers with expertise in quantum computing, quantum algorithms, and quantum information theory (on-site or visiting researchers and postdocs).
  3. Graduate Students: Students pursuing research in quantum computing and quantum information science.
  4. Software Developers: Programmers with strong (scientific, graphics, ... etc.) coding skills.
  5. Undergraduate Students: College and university students interested in computing, information science, or mathematics.
  6. (Future) Technical Staff: Experts in quantum computing, software development, and experimental research.
Faculty members will be well-versed in some quantum technology, with particular focus on quantum computing, quantum algorithms, quantum informatics, and related mathematical sciences.
Student members and software developers of QI Lab will be qualified and experienced in either graphics programming (e.g., with experience in 2D, 3D, VR, and visual interactive web apps programming) or have some strong scientific programming experience (e.g., Julia, R, Python, TensorFlow, Matlab, ... etc.), or they will have strong credentials in some related mathematical field (e.g., linear algebra, group theory, graph theory, category theory, ...). Knowledge of quantum algorithms research is a plus, but not a prerequisite, for students and developers joining QI Lab.

Resources and Requirements

At this initial stage of its development, QI Lab will need few resources, including few devices (e.g., two high-end laptops, two VR headsets) and few personnel (e.g., one or two members of IRI’s research staff, and/or interested members–students or faculty–of other institutes, such as Zewail City for Science and Technology, EUI, AIU, Alexandria University, AAST, ... etc.). QI Lab currently has no other financial requirements.
For electronic communication, QI Lab will have an email account (such as qilab@srtacity.sci.eg), and, preferably (later on), its own subdomain name (such as @qilab.srtacity.sci.eg). It will also have specialized social media (e.g., WhatsApp, Facebook, ... etc.) groups and accounts for sharing news, collaborating, and having online meetings (e.g., Zoom, Google Meet, ... etc.).
In the future, QI Lab may need and house resources and facilities such as:
  1. Quantum simulation software (e.g., Qiskit, Cirq, Q#, or QAL).
  2. High-performance classical computing resources.
  3. Collaboration tools and meeting spaces (with digital whiteboards, QAL access).
  4. XR/AR/VR and Gamepad setups for visual and interactive learning tools.
  5. Remote access to quantum cloud services (e.g., IBM, Amazon Braket).
  6. Quantum computing hardware (e.g., IBM Quantum, Rigetti Computing, or custom-built systems).

Budget

The budget of QI Lab will cover:
  1. Personnel and Staffing: Salaries and benefits for principal investigators, postdoctoral researchers, graduate students, and software developers (Based on institution rates).
  2. Research and Development (Operational funds): Funding for research projects, workshops, cloud access, publication, and conferences.
  3. Equipment and Resources: Quantum computing hardware, XR/AR/VR equipment, high-performance computing resources, software tools, and meeting spaces.

Expected Outcomes and Impact

The anticipated benefits and results of QI Lab will include:
  1. Producing published research in the fields of quantum informatics and quantum algorithms (these publications will present breakthroughs in quantum information science, and the development of new quantum algorithms and applications),
  2. Producing registered patents for novel technologies invented for teaching and researching quantum informatics, and
  3. Producing talented quantum software and quantum algorithm developers (supporting the creation of quantum tech startups and partnerships).
The lab will also offer free (and paid) versions of visual and interactive (classical) software (e.g., web apps) for teaching and researching quantum informatics and quantum algorithms and related mathematical fields (such as linear algebra, group theory, and graph theory).
The lab hopes to strongly foster research and interest in quantum informatics and quantum algorithms inside Egypt, and outside. Expected impact of the lab will be to position the institution (IRI) as a quantum research and education leader, attract research funding and attract student talent (supporting innovative startups). It will also enable original contributions to the global quantum ecosystem (enhancing the institution’s role in shaping the quantum future of Egypt, its surrounding regions, and the world).
QI Lab represents a timely and strategic investment in the future of computing. By combining rigorous science, educational innovation, and cutting-edge technology, QI Lab will contribute significantly to advancing Egypt’s and the region’s presence in the global quantum ecosystem. This reinforces the strategic importance of the lab and its alignment with institutional and national innovation goals.

Key Sections of a Lab Building Proposal
Executive Summary: A concise overview of the entire project, including the need, objectives, and key aspects of the proposal.
Introduction: Background information about the lab, the problem it solves, and the significance of the proposed project.
Needs Assessment: Clearly defines the problem or need that the new laboratory will address.
Goals and Objectives: Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, and time-bound (SMART) goals that the laboratory aims to achieve.
Scope of Work and Project Description: A detailed description of the lab, its purpose, and the work to be done, including preliminary designs, site evaluations, and any required soil testing.
 
Resources and Requirements: A comprehensive list of all necessary equipment, materials, and personnel.

Budget: A thorough breakdown of all project costs, including an estimated project cost.
Expected Outcomes: The anticipated benefits and results of the new laboratory.
 
Payment Schedule: Details on how and when payments will be made to contractors.
