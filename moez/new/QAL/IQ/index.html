What is QAL?

QAL is not “a program for running or executing quantum algorithms” in the usual sense. Rather, it is a visual, interactive environment that helps you experiment (“play”) with quantum algorithms and understand their mathematical foundations.

What exactly is QAL?

The concept of QAL can roughly be expressed as it being a Quantum Algorithms Lab, i.e., a lab for doing experiments on quantum algorithms—hence the name!

More precisely, QAL is a “virtual playground”—i.e., a virtual laboratory in which you interact with quantum algorithms in a visual way, instead of having to write quantum code or to deal only with abstract mathematical formulas.

What makes QAL particularly distinctive is that it tries to let you “touch” mathematics, interact with it, and visually see the effect of changing it. For example, instead of simply writing the equation

|Ψ'⟩ = U|Ψ⟩
to indicate that the quantum operation U transforms the state Ψ into the state Ψ', and treating it as an abstract equation, QAL tries to show you what happens to the vectors, matrices, amplitudes, and phases during the execution of quantum operations.

Why is QAL different from an ordinary quantum simulator?

This is a very important point. The program’s author, Dr. Moez Adel AbdelGawad, explains that QAL is not a quantum simulator in the traditional sense, and it should not be considered a replacement for conventional quantum computing simulators.

A traditional simulator often has you work at a level such as

Qubit → Gate → Circuit → Measurement
whereas QAL tries to go down to a deeper level
Quantum algorithm → matrices → vectors → complex amplitudes → mathematical structure.

In other words, you can study the internal mathematical structure of the algorithm itself. For this reason, the author uses a nice analogy: interacting with the components of a quantum algorithm is as if you were working with Lego pieces or a Rubik’s Cube—you change things, experiment, and see what happens.

What does Linear Algebra have to do with it?

This is the most important point for understanding QAL.

The operations of a quantum algorithm can be mathematically represented using complex-valued matrices, while a quantum state can be represented using complex-valued vectors. When several quantum operations are placed one after another, their mathematical composition becomes matrix multiplication.

Therefore, QAL does not aim to teach you Quantum Computing independently of mathematics. Instead, it tries to make you see the relationship between the two:

Quantum Computing → Quantum Algorithms → Linear Algebra → Vectors + Matrices + Complex Numbers → Group Theory / Graph Theory.

The research paper introducing QAL specifically mentions linear algebra, group theory, and graph theory as fundamental areas of mathematics that QAL attempts to present visually and interactively.

What can you do inside QAL?

According to the research paper introducing the program, there are two main levels of interaction with QAL.

The higher-level interaction deals with

  • Qubits
  • Quantum gates
  • Quantum circuits
  • Algorithms
This is similar to what you find in other quantum computing tools.

The deeper mathematical level, which is the distinctive part of QAL, deals with

  • Vectors
  • Matrices
  • Complex numbers
  • Quantum amplitudes
  • Magnitudes
  • Phases
  • Permutations
  • Groups
  • Graphs
The interface attempts to represent these concepts using images, colors, and interactive graphics, allowing you to see the relationships rather than merely reading about them in a book.

What does “playing with quantum algorithms” mean?

This is at the heart of the QAL philosophy.

Instead of learning through

Read the theory → memorize the equation → write the program → run it,
the idea becomes
Experiment → change → observe → notice a pattern → form a hypothesis → test it → understand the mathematics.

The paper calls this approach “calculated trial and error” or “what-if experimentation”—that is, carrying out deliberate experiments to discover what happens when you change a part of the algorithm.

This makes QAL more like an interactive mathematics laboratory than merely a software simulator.

Who is QAL intended for?

The paper identifies two main groups

For education:

  • School students, particularly STEM students
  • Undergraduate students
  • Computer Science students
  • Mathematics students
  • Physics students
For research:
  • Graduate students
  • Researchers
  • People working in fields related to mathematics and quantum computing
The idea of visual interaction with vectors and matrices is not limited to quantum computing. The paper points out that the way QAL presents linear algebra could also be useful in fields such as
  • Machine Learning / AI
  • Computer Graphics
  • Cybersecurity
  • Robotics
  • Mathematical Physics

QAL and QAL Lite

There are two editions of the program

QAL: The full edition, containing a larger collection of tools and interactive visualizations.
QAL Lite: A lighter edition available online, designed to provide a core part of the QAL experience in a simpler form.

The key takeaway

If you wanted to summarize QAL in one sentence: QAL is a visual, interactive environment that turns quantum algorithms and the mathematics underlying them into something you can explore and experiment with visually, rather than merely treating them as equations or code.

The central idea is not that QAL will do the work for you, but that it will help you develop intuition about why an algorithm works the way it does.

Another Description of QAL

QAL (Quantum Algorithms Lab) is an innovative, visual, and interactive tool, developed as a web application—a virtual playground—designed for experimenting with quantum algorithms and the mathematics underlying them, such as linear algebra, group theory, and graph theory.

The tool was created by Dr. Moez AbdelGawad of the Informatics Research Institute (IRI) at Egypt’s City of Scientific Research and Technological Applications (SRTA-City).

🎯 The Goal of QAL

QAL was designed to help students and researchers develop a deep and intuitive understanding of the mathematical properties of quantum algorithms, rather than limiting them to complex mathematical abstractions.

The program is based on the idea that the human brain is naturally oriented toward thinking in images. It therefore uses interactive graphics to make quantum concepts clearer and easier to understand.

✨ Main Features

Visual Presentations of Algorithms — QAL Algorithm Presentations

QAL presents a quantum algorithm as a sequence of matrices, with a visual representation of each step, while showing the overall operation on the far right.

The types of presentations include

  • Heatmaps
  • Quantum Circuit Diagrams
  • Other innovative visualizations

Direct Interaction

Users can select any element of a matrix or vector and directly change its phase and magnitude by moving or dragging graphical symbols, without having to write mathematical notation or coding.
The results are updated immediately, allowing users to see the effect of their changes on the overall operation.

Experimental Mathematics

QAL enables users to discover and verify mathematical facts visually and interactively.

For example, users can verify that applying a NOT operation followed by two QFT (Quantum Fourier Transform) operations is equivalent to an “add 1” operation (with a clockwise rotation).
They can also observe that multiplying a matrix on the right by NOT flips its columns, whereas multiplying it on the left flips its rows.

MVC Architecture and Web Technologies

QAL is built using HTML5/CSS and JavaScript/TypeScript. It uses the D3.js library for graphics and jQuery UI for interface controls.

🆚 QAL vs. QAL Lite

There are two editions of the tool
QAL Lite

  • A lightweight edition available for free online at the QAL Lite webpage.
  • Contains three basic interactive visualizations:
    • Heatmaps
    • Quantum circuits
    • A third visualization for textual matrix representations
  • Suitable for basic and educational use.

Full QAL

  • Includes everything in QAL Lite, plus seven advanced visualizations known as QA-LA NIVs.
  • QA-LA NIVs are (a group of) novel software technologies that can be registered for patent protection, and they add significant research and educational value by connecting quantum algorithms with graph theory.
  • Suitable for basic and advanced educational and research use.
The relationship between the two is somewhat like a regular car (QAL Lite) versus a luxury sports car (QAL): adding QA-LA NIVs to QAL Lite gives QAL considerably greater capabilities and range.

📚 Uses

In Education

QAL targets school students—especially students in STEM schools—as well as undergraduate students in Computer Science, Mathematics, and Physics.
It helps them overcome the difficulty of mathematical abstraction by allowing them to treat concepts such as matrices and vectors as tangible objects that they can experiment with and “play” with.

In Research

QAL can serve as a research assistant for quantum algorithms (QA RA), helping graduate students and researchers understand the structural properties of quantum algorithms.

This includes areas such as

  • Analysis of the eigenvalues (called the spectrum) and eigenvectors of quantum operations.
  • Studying the iterative/recursive (self-similar) structure of efficient quantum operations.
